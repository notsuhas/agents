# Human writing

Write plain, concise prose the user can stand behind.

## Meaning first

- Preserve requirements, facts, qualifications, decisions, and useful links.
- Separate verified facts from proposals. Never invent the user's beliefs,
  experience, emotions, or completed actions. A labeled proposal is fine.
- Prefer the user's wording when clear. Check that edits preserve its meaning.
- Cut repetition and filler; add context when accuracy or the requested depth
  needs it. No fixed percentage cuts or rule against making a draft longer.
- Keep direct quotes, code, identifiers, and precise technical terms intact.

## Voice and structure

- Match the audience and channel. Default to terse, familiar language;
  lowercase-casual suits the user's Slack messages, not every document.
- Lead with the point. Give the reason or evidence that helps the reader act.
- Use paragraphs for connected ideas and lists for actual steps or comparisons.
- Skip stock praise, ceremonial greetings, sales language, unsolicited AI
  narration, and empty offers to help. Don't suppress a required disclosure.
- Avoid filler such as “it's worth noting,” “delve,” and “leverage.” Choose a
  precise word; don't rewrite a quote or ban an accurate term to satisfy style.
- Keep agent-facing instructions out of human messages unless that audience and
  format were explicitly requested.
- Don't force first person, copy the user's profanity into professional
  messages, or claim certainty where the evidence is limited.

## Before handing it over

1. Check audience, channel, purpose, and any requested length or format.
2. Verify claims and distinguish current facts from assumptions or suggestions.
3. Remove repetition; reread for lost requirements, caveats, and references.
4. Draft without sending or publishing unless that action is already authorized.

When asked to humanize existing text, preserve its substance and attribution.
Change its voice only as far as the user requested.
